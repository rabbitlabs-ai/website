    Why

    One second of animation is up to 24 drawings. Almost every one is still drawn by hand.

    1,875

    artist-hours to draw one 22-minute TV episode.

    ~½

    of those hours go to the in-betweens, the drawings between the keys.

    4 for 3

    Our base case: give each animator 10 hours a week back and the same team makes four episodes in the time of three.

    How it works

    1. 01Your keys

      Load your key drawings as vector line art (SVG or Grease Pencil), with your exposure sheet. Holds and timing stay yours.

    2. 02Our drafts

      Our model matches each line between the keys and moves it along the motion, on the frames your sheet calls for.

    3. 03Your call

      Scrub with onion skin. Accept drawing by drawing, retake a single line, or fix it and lock it. Locked drawings are never touched.

    What stays yours

    Your timing.

    We draw on the frames your exposure sheet asks for. Holds, spacing and favours are the animator's decisions, not ours.

    Your lines.

    Output is vector, not pixels. Every line stays editable, so nobody redraws a frame for a director's note.

    Your building.

    The model runs on your own machines, and your art is never used for training without your permission.
